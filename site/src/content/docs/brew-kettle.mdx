The <Item id="brew_kettle" /> is where every drink starts. Put it on a heat source, add the ingredients, and a few seconds
later it holds a batch of juice, must or wort, ready to bottle. If you've ever cooked in Farmer's Delight's cooking pot, you
already know how it works.

## Heat

The kettle only brews while the block right below it is hot:

- **{heatNames(rules.heat.tray)}**: the kettle stands on an iron tray above them.
- **{heatNames(direct)}**: it sits right on top.
- **Through a {heatNames(rules.heat.conductors)}**: heat passes up through it, so a hopper can sit between the kettle and the
  heat source and still feed it.
- With Farmer's Delight, its **stove** heats the kettle too.

A campfire has to be lit. The little flame in the kettle's screen lights up when it's heated.

**Hang it up:** sneak and right-click the kettle with an empty hand to swap the tray for an iron handle, or place it against
the underside of a block. A hanging kettle still brews over a fire right below it, and looks great under a chain.

## Brewing

Open the kettle with a right-click and put the ingredients in. The gauge on the right shows the brew in its colour and how
full the kettle is; hover it for the servings.

<KettleScreen recipe="brewing/barley_wort" />

Each recipe makes **{rules.servingsPerBucket} servings** and takes **{seconds(brews[0].brewingTime)}**. The kettle keeps brewing
more batches as long as there are ingredients, up to {rules.kettleMaxServings} servings in total.

You can tell what a kettle is doing from across the room:

- **Brewing:** steam rises out of its chimney, it boils and bubbles. When a batch is done it lets out a puff of steam.
- **Done:** the steam stops and the tap drips now and then. The sight glass beside the tap shows how full it is.
- **On a lit fire with nothing to brew:** the fire's smoke goes up the chimney.

<BrewFlow />

The sight glass beside the tap shows the brew in its colour, sinking as you take servings out. While you have the kettle's
screen open, its lid is up and you can see the brew inside.

## Big kettles

<Render name="scenes/kettle-family" size={384} caption="A Big and a plain Brew Kettle" />

Place **8 kettles in a 2×2×2 cube** and they become one **Big Brew Kettle**. They join when you place the last kettle of
the cube. Kettles holding different brews, or of a different copper, don't join.

- **Bigger batches.** Every block adds a batch: a Big Kettle brews 32 servings at once. That's four barrels.
- **Fire.** One fire under it is enough. Every fire under its bottom layer makes it faster: with a fire under every block it
  brews a full batch in twice the time of a single kettle. Short on ingredients? It brews as
  many sets as the grid holds.
- **Room.** It holds 64 servings per block: 512 in a Big Kettle.
- **Any block.** Every block of it opens the screen, fills bottles and buckets, takes hoppers and gives a comparator signal.
- **Breaking.** Break any block and the kettle falls apart into single kettles, each keeping its share of the brew; the
  broken one drops with its share. Put a kettle back into the gap and it joins again.
- **One vessel.** It weathers as one, and one honeycomb or one axe scrape changes all of it.

## Chimney

<Render name="scenes/kettle-chimney" float="right" size={192} alt="A kettle whose chimney goes through a plank roof" />

Right-click a kettle or its chimney with a <Item id="copper_ingot" /> and the chimney grows a block taller, up to 8 blocks. A
roof in its way stays whole: the pipe goes through up to two roof blocks (stairs, slabs, planks or any solid block) and the
next piece sits on top. A thicker roof or a block that holds things, like a chest, stops it. The chimney ages with its
kettle. Mine a piece and it comes down with everything above it, giving the ingots back; breaking the kettle does the same.
A hanging kettle has no room for one.

## Bottling

Like a cauldron: right-click the kettle with an empty container.

- a <Item id="glass_bottle" /> takes **{rules.servingsPerBottle} serving**,
- an empty <Item id="bucket" /> takes **{rules.servingsPerBucket} servings**, so the kettle needs at least that many.

Buckets of juice and wort can't be poured out into the world, like milk. They go into a cauldron, though: a bucket
fills it, and empty bottles take it out again one serving at a time. See [Interactions](/interactions/#cauldrons).

## Juice to drink

<Item id="grape_juice" />, <Item id="white_grape_juice" />, <Item id="sweet_berry_juice" /> and <Item id="apple_juice" /> are
drinks on their own: they take {juice.consumable.seconds} s to drink, you can always drink them, and they fill a little hunger.

Every juice also lowers [Tipsy](/tipsy/) by one level. Wort and honey must have to ferment first; you can't drink them.

## Automation

Hoppers can run the kettle on their own:

- **From above**, a hopper feeds ingredients in, and empty bottles or buckets.
- **From the sides**, it feeds empty bottles or buckets.
- The kettle fills them from the brew, and **a hopper below** pulls out the filled ones.

Nothing pulls the ingredients or the brew itself out.

A comparator next to the kettle gives a signal as soon as anything is inside, ingredients, brew or containers, and a
stronger one the fuller all of it gets. A brew on its own only gives a weak signal, so use it to tell "something in
there" from "empty" rather than to count servings.

## Copper

The kettle is copper, and it ages like copper.

<Stages prefix="kettle-weathering" title="Weathering" labels={['Copper', 'Exposed', 'Weathered', 'Oxidized']} />

- Over time it turns exposed, weathered and finally oxidized. It brews the same at every stage.
- **Honeycomb** waxes it and stops the weathering. An **axe** scrapes off wax or one stage of weathering, and lightning cleans it.
- **Break it full** and it keeps its brew: the item's tooltip and fill bar show it, and you can place it somewhere else
  and carry on. Ingredients and waiting bottles or buckets drop on the ground.
- It doesn't weather while someone has its screen open (a single kettle).

## Good to know

- **Lost the fire?** Taking the heat away doesn't reset a batch, but the arrow slips back twice as fast as it filled.
  Ingredients are only used up when a batch finishes, so nothing is wasted.
- **One brew at a time.** The kettle won't brew a different drink on top of a brew that's still inside: it waits, with the
  ingredients untouched, until you've bottled the old one out. At {rules.kettleMaxServings} servings it waits too.
- **Keep the grid clean.** Any item the recipe doesn't use stops the kettle, leftovers from another recipe included. A
  hopper on top pushes in whatever it holds, so feed it only ingredients.
- **Where's my XP?** Each batch earns a little experience. The kettle saves it up and gives it to the next player who fills
  a bottle or bucket by hand, or drops it when it's broken.
- **Not enough for a bucket?** A click with a bucket just opens the screen. Sneaking with a bottle or bucket does nothing.
- **One hopper, two jobs:** a hopper under the kettle passes the campfire's heat up and collects the filled containers.
- **Sneak and right-click with an empty hand** to hang the kettle from its handle, and again to stand it back up.
- The kettle can stand in water, it breaks quickly with any tool (a pickaxe is fastest), keeps its anvil name, and doesn't
  stack. An explosion can destroy it, brew and all.
- **Recipe book:** brews unlock when you pick up their main crop, sorted into Juices, Worts and Misc.
