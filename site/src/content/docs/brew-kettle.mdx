The <Item id="brew_kettle" /> is where every drink starts. Put it on a heat source, add the ingredients, and a few seconds
later it holds a batch of juice or wort, ready to bottle. If you've ever cooked in Farmer's Delight's cooking pot, you
already know how it works.

## Heat

The kettle only brews while the block right below it is hot:

- **{heatNames(rules.heat.tray)}**: the kettle stands on an iron tray above them.
- **{heatNames(direct)}**: it sits right on top.
- **Through a {heatNames(rules.heat.conductors)}**: heat passes up through it, so a hopper can sit between the kettle and the
  heat source and still feed it.
- With Farmer's Delight, its **stove** heats the kettle too.

A campfire has to be lit. The little flame in the kettle's screen lights up when it's heated.

**Hang it up:** sneak and right-click the kettle with an empty hand to swap the tray for an iron handle, or place it against
the underside of a block. A hanging kettle still brews over a fire right below it, and looks great under a chain.

## Brewing

Open the kettle with a right-click and put the ingredients in. The gauge on the right shows the brew in its colour and how
full the kettle is; hover it for the servings.

<KettleScreen recipe="brewing/barley_wort" />

Each recipe makes **{rules.servingsPerBucket} servings** and takes **{seconds(brews[0].brewingTime)}**. While it brews, steam
curls out from under the lid and the lid rattles; when it's done, it lets out a puff of steam. The kettle keeps brewing
more batches as long as there are ingredients, up to {rules.kettleMaxServings} servings in total.

Once a brew is done, the lid stays up and you can see it inside, sinking as you take servings out. While a batch brews, the
lid is down; when the last serving is gone, it closes.

## Bottling

Like a cauldron: right-click the kettle with an empty container.

- a <Item id="glass_bottle" /> takes **{rules.servingsPerBottle} serving**,
- an empty <Item id="bucket" /> takes **{rules.servingsPerBucket} servings**, so the kettle needs at least that many.

Buckets of juice and wort can't be poured out into the world, like milk.

## Juice to drink

<Item id="grape_juice" /> and <Item id="sweet_berry_juice" /> are drinks on their own: they take {juice.consumable.seconds} s to
drink, you can always drink them, and they fill a little hunger.

<FoodStats nutrition={juice.food.nutrition} saturation={juice.food.saturation} />

Grape juice also lowers [Tipsy](/tipsy/) by one level. Wort has to ferment first; you can't drink it.

## Automation

Hoppers can run the kettle on their own:

- **From above**, a hopper feeds ingredients in, and empty bottles or buckets.
- **From the sides**, it feeds empty bottles or buckets.
- The kettle fills them from the brew, and **a hopper below** pulls out the filled ones.

Nothing pulls the ingredients or the brew itself out.

A comparator next to the kettle reads how full it is.

## Copper

The kettle is copper, and it ages like copper.

<Stages prefix="kettle-weathering" title="Weathering" labels={['Copper', 'Exposed', 'Weathered', 'Oxidized']} />

- Over time it turns exposed, weathered and finally oxidized. It brews the same at every stage.
- **Honeycomb** waxes it and stops the weathering. An **axe** scrapes off wax or one stage of weathering, and lightning cleans it.
- **Break it full** and it keeps its contents: the item's tooltip shows the brew, and you can place it somewhere else and carry on.
